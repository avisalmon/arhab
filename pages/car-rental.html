🚗 Car Rental

What car to rent, where to pick it up, when to book, and what actually drives the price. This is a first research pass — figures below are general/ballpark; once dates, group size, and route are locked in the trip doc, this page gets tightened up with real quotes.

To finalize this page we need: number of travelers + luggage, pickup city/airport, drop-off city (same or different?), exact dates, and drivers' ages. Pull these from the trip notes doc once settled.

What class of car to rent

Pick the class by group size + luggage, not by budget alone — an undersized car on a multi-week road trip gets old fast. Rule of thumb: count real suitcases, not seats.

GroupLuggageSuggested classNotes
1–2 people 1–2 bags Compact / Midsize Cheapest to rent and to fuel; fine for cities + highway.
3–4 people 3–4 bags Full-size / Standard SUV Trunk space is the real constraint, not seats — check trunk liters, not just "5-seater".
4–5 people Lots of gear (ski/beach/hiking) Large SUV (e.g. Explorer/Highlander class) Worth it if doing national parks or winter driving — AWD/4WD available.
6–7+ people Any Minivan (Pacifica/Odyssey class) Best interior space per dollar for big families; sliding doors help at every stop.
Rental "classes" (Economy → Compact → Midsize → Full-size → SUV → Minivan → Premium) are not standardized between companies — the actual model you get is "or similar," so treat the class as a size/trunk guarantee, not a specific car.

Where to rent from

OptionProsCons
Airport counter Land and drive; widest fleet choice; easy one-way returns to another airport. Airport concession fees + taxes commonly add 15–30% to the rate.
Off-airport / downtown branch Noticeably cheaper (no airport fees); sometimes better upgrade availability. Need a taxi/rideshare to get there; limited hours (often closed evenings/Sundays).
Peer-to-peer (Turo, etc.) Unique/newer vehicles, sometimes cheaper, delivery to your door possible. Owner cancellations happen; insurance is via the platform, not standard rental CDW; less predictable for multi-city trips.

One-way trips (pick up in one city, drop off in another): confirm the company allows it on the route and check the one-way / drop fee early — it can be anywhere from $0 (same state, common route) to several hundred dollars (different state, less common route, or across a distant region).

Prices swing more by city, date, and how far in advance you book than by company — always compare 2–3 companies for the same dates rather than assuming one is "the cheap one."

When to book

  • Book early for peak season (summer, major holidays, national-park months May–Sept) — fleets sell out in popular gateway cities and prices climb closer to the date.
  • Re-check the price after booking. Most major companies let you cancel/rebook a prepaid-free reservation at no cost if the rate drops — set a reminder to check again 2–3 weeks and again a few days before pickup.
  • Prefer "pay at pickup / free cancellation" over prepaid rates while dates could still shift, even if prepaid looks a bit cheaper.
  • Avoid booking at the very last minute in smaller airports/cities — the fleet (and the good vehicles) can simply run out.

What actually drives the total cost

Line itemWatch for
Base daily rateCompare like-for-like class and same pickup/drop-off combo.
Insurance / CDW / LDWCheck first whether your credit card or home car/travel insurance already covers a US rental — can save a lot; otherwise budget for it, it's rarely optional in practice for a road trip.
One-way / drop feeSee "Where to rent" above — can be the single biggest surprise cost.
Young driver feeUnder-25 (sometimes under-21) drivers get a daily surcharge or restricted classes at most companies.
Additional driver feeSome companies waive it for spouses/domestic partners — ask.
Fuel policy"Full-to-full" is almost always cheapest — return it full and fill up just before drop-off, never prepay fuel unless the tank is genuinely hard to refill nearby.
TollsRental toll transponders often charge a daily fee even on no-toll days — compare against paying tolls directly (license-plate billing / EZPass) for the route. See Navigation & Info.
Taxes & airport surchargesCan be 15–30%+ on top of the quoted rate at airports — always check the "total due" price, not the daily rate shown first.
Cross-border restrictionsIf the route crosses into Canada/Mexico, confirm the rental agreement allows it — not all do, and insurance may not extend across the border.

Booking checklist

  • ☐ Confirm group size + luggage → pick a class (see table above)
  • ☐ Decide pickup/drop-off cities — same city or one-way?
  • ☐ Compare 2–3 companies for the same dates/locations
  • ☐ Check if existing credit card / insurance covers CDW before buying the rental's
  • ☐ Book a free-cancellation rate, not prepaid, while dates can still move
  • ☐ Re-check price ~2–3 weeks out and again a few days before pickup
  • ☐ Decide on rental toll pass vs. paying tolls directly
  • ☐ Note driver ages (young-driver fee?) and who else will drive
  • ☐ Screenshot/save the confirmation + full "total due" breakdown
